[[Leonhard Euler|Euler's]] Formula defines a fundamental relationship between the exponential function $e^{ix}$ and the trigonometric functions $\sin(x)$ and $\cos(x)$. It essentially shows that in the complex plane, an exponential function of $x$ and $i$ geometrically represents an oscillation between the real number $x$ and the imaginary number $i$ which can be equate to real oscillation identical to $\cos$ added to an imaginary oscillation identical to a $\sin$ function.
# Definition
$$
e^{i \theta}=\cos(\theta)+i \sin(\theta)
$$

## Euler's Identity
A natural consequence of this formula is the following equation, derived by rearranging the terms of the formula, 
$$
e^{i \theta}=\cos(\theta)+i \sin(\theta)
$$
$$
\text{if} \>\> \theta=\pi \to e^{i\pi}=\cos(\pi)+i\sin(\pi)
$$
$$
\cos(\pi)=-1
$$
$$
\sin(\pi)=0
$$
$$
\therefore \>\>e^{i\pi}=-1 \>\> \text{or}\>\>e^{i\pi}+1=0
$$
