Power from the grid can be messy and dangerous. Many forms of EMI (electromagnetic inteference) or power surges can fry your circuit if it is left unprotected. Therefore, a necessary entry point in any robust power circuit is the filtering stage, which blocks all kinds of extreme events in the grid from disrupting or destroying your circuit.

All circuit designers make certain assumptions about how the incoming power should look. For a DC microcontroller, it expects flat and constant 5V DC. For a power converter like a phone charging adapter, it expects the wall power to be split-phase 60Hz 120V RMS
## Components of an EMI Filter
A filter designer must think of two things before he starts: what kinds of electrical signal should be allowed through (with all others being denied entry), and what characteristics make up those allowed signals. In a power conversion circuit connected to U.S. mains power, the designer would want the AC power which enters his circuit to closely match the shape of what ideal mains power should look like, that is, a 2-phase, 120 V RMS, 60 Hz waveform. A filter must take whatever happens to be present in the mains power outlet and convert it to match this ideal waveform. Therefore, the designer must consider, 
- amplitude, 
- frequency
